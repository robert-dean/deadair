# Changelog

Notable changes to the iOS listener, newest first. Its version is `MARKETING_VERSION` in
`Config/Version.xcconfig`, mirrored there from this directory's `package.json` by
`pnpm release:version`; entries are written from changesets naming `@deadair/ios`. A version is
uploaded to TestFlight by the iOS release workflow, from an `ios-v<version>` tag or by hand, and the
station's own changes are in the [root changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md).

## [Unreleased]

## [0.8.3] — 2026-10-10

- Signed in, Now playing has a Lyrics button whenever the record you are hearing has words. It opens the lyrics with the line being sung lit and kept in view, timed to what you hear rather than to what the station has just sent, and shows the words as they came when the source has no timings. Signed out, or on an instrumental or a talk break, there is no button.

## [0.8.2] — 2026-10-10

- Now playing has a Request button for anybody signed in, so asking the station for a record no longer means going to Up next first. It sits at the end of the play controls, with the operator's heart moved from beside the title to the other end, and Request has a new icon everywhere it appears: a speech bubble with a note in it.

## [0.8.1] — 2026-10-10

- Now playing changes record when you hear it change. While the station was playing on your phone, the screen and the player bar moved to the next record as soon as the station picked it, several seconds before the music did and before the lock screen did, and the progress bar ran the same few seconds ahead. They now follow the audio you are hearing, as the lock screen does. When a record changes before the app has heard about it, the lock screen no longer puts the previous record back up for a moment; it asks the station and shows the new one. A cover that takes a moment to load is no longer dropped when the station has already moved on to the next record. With the station stopped, the screen shows what the station is playing, as before.

## [0.8.0] — 2026-10-05

- You can now start the station with Siri, a Shortcut or the Action button ("Play deadair"), even after you have swiped the app away. The app also has a CarPlay screen: the station as a single row, showing the record on air (or who is on the mic, with their picture during a break), and pressing it plays the station and opens the usual Now Playing screen. The CarPlay screen turns on once Apple approves it for the app, so for now this update changes nothing in the car.

## [0.7.0] — 2026-10-05

- When the presenter on air has a picture, the app now shows it. On Now playing it sits beside their name under the record, and on Up next beside the host's name at the top. While the presenter is talking between records, their picture takes the place of the cover on Now playing, in the player bar and on the lock screen, and the page takes its colours from it. A presenter without a picture, or a station that does not send one yet, looks exactly as before.

## [0.6.0] — 2026-10-04

- Signed in, you can now ask the station to play a record. Up next has a Request button: search by title or artist, pick a record, and add your name, a dedication or a message if you like. When the station holds few matches, records from its music providers are offered too, marked with where they come from, and the station fetches one before playing it. The station plays a request soon after it is asked for, between two records, if its rules allow, and it takes one request at a time from each listener. The same page lists your recent requests and what became of each one, including the station's reason when it turned one down.

## [0.5.0] — 2026-10-03

- A new format, Automatic, is now what the app plays unless you pick one: HLS where the station publishes it, so moving between wifi and mobile data does not cut the stream, and MP3 where it does not. After updating, an app that was on MP3 moves to Automatic once, because the app never recorded whether MP3 was chosen or just the default; pick MP3 again under Settings to keep it. Any other format you chose is kept.
- Moving between wifi and mobile data no longer leaves the stream on the network it started on. An MP3, AAC, Opus or FLAC stream is reconnected over the new network the moment the phone switches, and a reconnect that was already waiting is made at once. HLS, which fetches each piece of audio afresh, is left as it is.
- A stream that stops delivering audio without the player reporting a failure is now reconnected. It used to wait indefinitely while the app said it was playing; now ten seconds of silence after audio has been heard (thirty while the station is starting up) counts as a dropped stream, and it is retried as one.
- A stream that drops while the phone has no network now waits for one instead of retrying against nothing. It used to say it could not reach the stream after five minutes, even when the signal came back seconds later; now it reconnects the moment a network is back, starting again from a short wait, and gives up only after fifteen minutes without one.

## [0.4.0] — 2026-10-03

- Save a talk break as well as sharing it. Every row in "What it said" that has a share button now has a save button beside it, which fetches the same small copy and opens the Files save dialog, so the clip can be kept on the phone, in iCloud Drive or wherever you pick. It asks for no permission.

## [0.3.0] — 2026-10-02

- The station's operator can add a record from the library to Up next, as on Android: Manage's Add a record searches by title once two letters are typed, and each result offers Play next or Add to the end. A record the station has no audio for says so instead. A record's page offers the same two buttons. The station still decides, and a record it will not play now says so.
- The station's operator can put a playlist or a chart on air from Manage, as on Android. Playlists are grouped by the plugin that offers them, and a plugin that could not be asked says so beside the others. A playlist page lists its tracks, and a chart page lists its records with rank, peak and weeks, and asks which way round to play them: a countdown ending on number one, number one first, or no fixed order. Either page can take calls during the broadcast. Airing asks once, because it replaces what every listener hears.
- Record, album and artist pages, for a signed-in account, as on Android. A record's page shows its cover, credits, year and length, every time the station aired it, and what the enrichment providers say about it: tags, release details, the station's own claims with the source each came from, facts, a biography and links, and which provider said what and when. The credit opens the artist and the album opens the album, whose tracks open their records. Every record in Up next and in Played opens its page.
- Now playing and Up next wear the on-air cover's colours, as on Android: the controls take the cover's most colourful colour, lightened or darkened to read on the page, and four soft blobs of its colours sit behind the page. On Now playing they drift while the station is on air, and on Up next they stand still at the top of the list. A black-and-white cover keeps the app's green controls over a grey mesh. With Reduce Motion on, nothing moves.
- The desk, for the station's operator, opened from Settings as on Android. It says whether the station is on air and to how many listeners, with Skip beside Take off air. Take off air needs a second press within five seconds, and a draining bar shows the time left. Start appears in its place when the station is stood down. The desk also offers holding the broadcast against the schedule (past the next block, or for two hours) and releasing it again, and the air mode (when someone listens, or always). At the bottom is the station's own account of why it can or cannot be heard: what is blocking it, any other faults, a remedy to copy, and every check it ruled out. The panel opens by itself when there is a fault.
- The station's operator can rearrange Up next. Edit puts handles on the records still to come, which can be dragged to a new place. Nothing can be dropped among the records the player already holds, and the list keeps the new order while the station answers. A long press on a row offers Play next, Move up and Move down, wherever each would change something. It is also how VoiceOver moves a row. Swipe or the same menu drops a row, and a dropped record can be put back where it was from the message that follows.
- The lock screen, Control Center, headphones and a car's next button now skip the record for the station's operator, as they do on Android. A listener's controls still offer no next button, and the button comes and goes with the account's role, whether or not the app is on screen. One press at a time reaches the station. The same control means "next track" in every other app, so a passenger reaching for it cuts the record for everybody listening.
- Manage and Plan for the station's operator, as on Android. Up next's corner button opens Manage, which holds Replan the show and What it said; a signed-in listener's button still opens What it said. Plan can keep this show and have the station programme what is still to come again, optionally against new words, or start a new show from words, with a host, a period, a mode, what happens when it runs out, and phone-ins. Starting a new show over a running one asks first. The page closes once the station has taken the plan.
- Now playing takes Android's layout. The cover runs the full width and fades into the page's colours, with a large title, the credit and who is presenting under it, a hairline playhead, and a lit play button between Shuffle and Skip. Left alone while playing, the tabs slide away and then the screen gives itself to the cover; the first touch brings everything back. The sleep timer moves to Settings, under Listening.
- The station's operator can rate what the station said, as on Android. Every attempt in What it said that produced words has three choices: the station should not say things like this, heard it with no opinion, or more like this. An attempt nobody has rated shows nothing pressed. Nothing acts on a script rating yet; the station keeps it.
- The station's operator can rate records, albums and artists from their pages, as on Android: never play it, no opinion, or play it more often, with a line under the control saying this is the station's mark and not a favourite. Now playing has a heart beside the title for the record on air, which likes it or takes the like back. Disliking stays on the record's page, where a thumb cannot reach it by accident.
- The station's operator can change who presents the show from the host's name at the top of Up next, as on Android. The sheet lists the station's characters, excluding callers, who can never be put on air. It marks the one presenting now and offers handing back to the station's own host. A note says that the breaks already written for the show are written again. The sheet closes once the station has taken the change.
- Signing in has a page of its own, as on Android. Every Sign in opens it, from Settings, a signed-out tab, or setup, and it closes itself once the station says yes. It has a way to show the password, and the code step is headed "One more step". Signing out asks first. In Settings, each format says what it is for, and one the station does not publish is greyed with the reason. Play when the app opens and the sleep timer explain themselves in their own rows. Changing the station's address in Settings offers "Use" rather than "Listen to".
- Share a talk break from What it said, as the Android app can. Every attempt whose words became audio has a share button, which asks the station for its small copy of that break (sized so a text message can carry it) and opens the share sheet with it. The app keeps only the one copy it is sending. A station older than this release sends the full-size original instead, which still shares but may be too big for a text message.
- Skip and Shuffle for the station's operator, either side of the play button on Now playing, as on Android. One command goes at a time, and both wait while there is nothing on air. The station's answer shows at once and the screen settles over the next couple of seconds as the change reaches the stream. For a signed-in account the cover now opens the record on air, and Up next has a button for everything the station has said. The player bar is now only over What's on, as on Android: Up next already shows what is playing in its own row.
- `deadair://` links open the app, as they do the Android and desktop apps: the console's `deadair://connect?station=…` and the `deadair://radio.example.com` shorthand. A link only ever proposes a station. On a fresh install it fills the setup field; with a station already kept it asks over the app, and nothing changes until the new address has answered and you press Listen. "Keep the station I have" turns it down, and a link to the station already kept asks nothing.
- The app is now tabs, as the Android app is: Now playing, What's on and Settings, each keeping its own place when you switch away and back. The player bar is along the bottom of every tab but Now playing, and tapping its record goes back to Now playing; its button still plays and stops. Played opens from Now playing, as before.
- Up next takes Android's layout. Who is presenting heads the page over the on-air cover's colours, with Manage beside them, and there is no title bar. The way to what played earlier sits above the list instead of scrolling away with it. The record on air sits on a card of its own, rows are larger with no lines between them, and each row the operator can change has a ⋮ button holding the moves, Drop, and Move rows. The host picker is a sheet of radio choices.
- Up next, for a signed-in account: the running order as the station holds it, opening on the record on air, with what has already played folded behind a count ("12 played earlier, 2 skipped") that opens with a tap. Each row says where it has got to, a break that will not air says why, and the host is named at the top. Played now opens from here, from "Everything the station has played", as on Android, rather than from Now playing.
- A welcome screen before the address field, as on Android: the station's mark and name, Find your station, and the privacy policy, which is now reachable before a station has been named and from Settings. Once an address has answered, setup offers "I run this station: sign in" beside Listen, which keeps the station and opens Settings to sign in.
- What it said, for a signed-in account: a break in Up next now opens every attempt the station made at it, newest first, each with when it was, whether it was written, declined or failed, who wrote it (the model or the floor), and the words, or the station's reason where there were none. A tap shows the rest: the kind, the host, the model, how long it took and the records either side.
- Play and stop no longer wait on the audio session on the main thread. Starting and ending the session waits on the system's media server, and iOS flagged it as a hang risk on every play and stop, measured on a phone; a slow media server could freeze the screen for as long as it took. The station still takes over audio as a music app does, and the silent switch still does not mute it.
- History and What it said take Android's layout. History is called History, its rows are Android's, and a stale list fades only its covers. Both lists end in an Earlier button rather than loading as you reach the foot. The script rating and the record rating are Android's segmented row: an outlined pill with the chosen answer filled.
- Played lists one record per row again. Every record had been stacked into a single row, and a record's airings and What it said had the same fault. Up next now opens with the "played earlier" count in view above the record on air instead of scrolled out of sight, and the player bar stays along the bottom while a tab is loading.
- The record, album and artist pages take Android's layout: one scrolling page with headings instead of grouped boxes, what the providers say on an outlined card with tags that wrap, and the details laid out as label-over-value cells. Play next and Add to the end share the width as outlined buttons. On an artist's page, who they are now comes before their albums, and an artist with no albums says so.
- Setup says how to use the code the station's console shows: point the iPhone's Camera at it, and deadair opens with the address filled in. The app asks for no camera access to do it.
- The screens say "signed out", "could not reach the station" and "nothing here yet" the way Android does: a heading, a line under it, and a Try again button where trying again can help, instead of a line asking you to pull down. A screen showing an old reading says when it was read, and stays readable rather than dimmed. The app's green is Android's: deeper in light mode, brighter in dark.
- What's on takes Android's layout: the station's name as its title, each block on an outlined card with its name kept to two lines, and "Nothing scheduled" between blocks. The player bar along its foot matches Android's too, with a spinner in the button while the station comes on air.

## [0.2.1] — 2026-10-02

- A refresh asked for while nothing was showing no longer makes the next screen ask the station twice when it opens.

## [0.2.0] — 2026-09-13

- A "Play when the app opens" switch in Settings, off by default. With it on, opening the app starts the station. Coming back to the app, or finishing setting up a new station, does not start it, and the first seconds are quiet while the station comes on air.
- Signed in, Now playing gains a Played button: what the station has played, newest first, with each record's cover and when it aired, and older pages as you scroll. It refreshes while it is open and asks the station nothing once it is closed.
- A player bar sits along the bottom of Settings, with the cover, what is on and the play or stop button, so the station can be stopped without going back to Now playing.
- Now playing names the show and who presents it, on a line above the record ("Late Static · with Cass"). While the station talks between records, the title says the host is on the mic and the break's own label goes underneath, where the artist would be; the lock screen and Control Center say the same instead of a title over nothing. It needs a station new enough to say what is on, and against an older one the screen looks exactly as it did.
- A sleep timer: while the station is playing, the moon under the play button stops it after 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, or at the end of the record that is on. The sound fades out over the last ten seconds, and the next press of play is at full volume. "After this record" waits for the end you actually hear, a few seconds after the station's own. The countdown shows beside the moon, and stopping by hand turns the timer off.
- Signed in, Now playing gains a What's on button: the block on now with how far through it is and who presents it, what is up next and after that, or how long the station has between blocks. If somebody has put something else on by hand, the block says "Due now" rather than "On air".
